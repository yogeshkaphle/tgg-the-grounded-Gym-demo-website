Fitness loves a tribe. The calisthenics crowd thinks lifting weights is cheating. The powerlifters think a pull-up is a warm-up. The kettlebell people have a swing for every problem. And somewhere, someone is doing an hour of stretching and calling it a workout.

I started in the first tribe. I learned my first muscle-up on a bar in a Patan park and, for a couple of years, I was sure bodyweight was the only honest way to train. Then I hurt my shoulder doing too much of one thing, and a powerlifter taught me how to get it strong again. That was the end of my tribe.

At Grounded Gym we treat each method as a tool. A good builder doesn't argue about whether the hammer is better than the saw. They ask what they are building.

## What each tool is good at

**Calisthenics** teaches you to control your own body. Pull-ups, push-ups, dips, handstands, and the steps that lead to them. It builds strength relative to your size, and it is the most portable training there is. A bar and a floor go almost anywhere.

**Kettlebells** build strength that moves. Swings, cleans and carries train your hips, grip and lungs at the same time. Real life rarely hands you a load that sits still, which is why forty-five minutes of kettlebell work is some of the most useful conditioning I know.

**Barbell strength** is the simplest way to get strong, and to know that you are getting strong. Squat, bench press, deadlift. The numbers go up slowly and honestly. We teach the powerlifting fundamentals because they are the clearest lesson in progression you will ever get. We don't care about competition totals unless you do.

**Mobility** is the range to use all of the above. Joint control, balance, breathing. It isn't stretching for its own sake. It's the reason your squat gets deeper and your back stops complaining.

## Your goal picks the mix

Here is the question we actually ask: what do you want to be ready for?

- **A trek next spring?** More kettlebell carries, step-ups and engine work, with some barbell strength for your legs.
- **A pull-up, finally?** Calisthenics first, with mobility for your shoulders.
- **Losing weight and keeping it off?** Kettlebells and conditioning, with barbell lifts so you keep your muscle.
- **Never trained and not sure?** Foundations, where you try a bit of each and find what you enjoy.

Over a year, most members use all four. The mix changes as the goal changes. That is the whole point of having a full toolbox.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

Tribes keep people training one way, often long after that way stopped working for them. Tools keep you asking a better question. The day your knee is sore, you have options. The week you're travelling, you have options. The month you plateau, you have options.
