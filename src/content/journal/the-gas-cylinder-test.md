Every gym has a test. Some weigh you. Some take a photo. Ours starts with a question: could you carry a full gas cylinder up to a fourth-floor flat without stopping?

Most people laugh. Then they think about it. Then they tell me about the last time they tried.

That is the point. The cylinder is not a party trick. It is a picture of what fitness is for. In Kathmandu, real life asks a lot of your body. Stairs with no lift. Footpaths that are half broken. A scooter you sometimes have to push. A child who wants to be carried up to the roof to fly a kite in Dashain. None of that happens in front of a mirror.

## What "ready" means

When we say we train people to be ready, we mean something you can check on an ordinary Tuesday:

- You can carry heavy, awkward things without your back paying for it the next day.
- You can walk uphill for an hour and still enjoy the view at the top.
- You can get down on the floor and back up without using your hands.
- You can play futsal on Friday and walk normally on Saturday.
- You can stay calm when a set gets hard, and finish it anyway.

None of those are about how you look. All of them change how you look, as it happens. Build strength and stamina, eat sensibly, sleep, and the mirror follows. It just isn't the thing we chase.

## Why not chase the mirror?

Because it is a bad coach. The mirror can't tell you if your knees are getting stronger. It won't notice that you stopped holding the railing on the stairs. On a bad-sleep day it lies to you, and on a good day it makes you think you can skip the work.

Numbers you can feel are better. We measure three in your first week:

1. **The carry.** A kettlebell in one hand, walk as far as you can with good posture, then swap hands. We note the weight and the distance.
2. **The step test.** Three minutes of stepping up and down, then we check how fast your heart rate settles.
3. **The floor test.** Sit down on the floor and stand back up. How many hands, knees and wobbles did it take?

We retest every few months. Nobody has ever cried over a step test. Plenty of people have been quietly proud of one.

## The steady part

There is a second half to the gas cylinder test, and it happens between your ears. Around the second floor, your legs start arguing. Your grip starts to go. The easiest thing is to put it down.

Training is where you practise the other choice. Not by suffering for the sake of it, because "no pain, no gain" gets people hurt. By choosing a hard thing that is the right size, doing it, and adding a little more next week.

Do that for a few months and something changes outside the gym too. The difficult conversation, the deadline, the long bus ride home in the monsoon: they start to feel like the fourth floor. Hard, known, and doable.

That is what we mean by a capable body and a steady mind. You get both from the same work.
